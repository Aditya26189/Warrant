# Limitations and known issues

This is the current assurance boundary. A favorable result in another chapter
does not override these limitations.

## FPR-001: adaptive returned-bound interpretation

The native adaptive returned numerical FPR bound does not support the general
coverage interpretation suggested by its name. A retained tied-score witness
demonstrates undercoverage of that interpretation.

The fixed requested-budget proposition is different. The witness does not
falsify it in that specified distribution. The complete code-to-proof mapping,
assumptions and consumers still need independent statistical/source review.

The [tracked issue](../../docs/research/fpr_reporting/issues/FPR-001-adaptive-bound.md)
describes repair candidates and compatibility questions. This phase does not
apply a behavioral repair or rewrite historical certificates.

## Historical source gaps

Some retained artifacts report dirty producing trees. The
[claim audit](claim-audit.md) identifies their exact rows and files. Their
commit hashes alone do not reconstruct the complete original source.

A model identifier also differs from an immutable weight identity. The
original loader/provenance path does not provide a complete historical weight
hash merely by naming a model. Frozen-score arithmetic remains reproducible;
full model-level reconstruction needs additional material.

## Labels and samples

Alias matching is an operational label rule, not human proof of answer truth.
Another rule or reviewer can expose disagreement without proving who is right.

Hand-authored challenges and positive enrichment do not establish a
representative customer distribution. Repeated forms retain their dependence.
Correct sample-size arithmetic cannot repair a biased sampling design.

The program validates declared IDs, values and supported group structure. It
does not discover all semantic duplicates, authenticate sampling or validate
a review rubric.

## Intervals and legacy replay

Percentile bootstrap intervals retain the assumptions and limitations of their
design. Numerical reconstruction does not establish their coverage.

Historical grouped boundary and hard-negative records preserve older inference.
New issuance restrictions do not retrospectively change those records'
meaning. Score replay also does not rerun the controls needed for warrant
issuance.

## Transfer and mechanisms

The long-context experiment uses constructed distractors and fixed original
questions, answers and labels. It evaluates representation transfer, not
fresh long-context generation accuracy.

Dilution, positional effects and extreme-value selection are mechanistic
hypotheses. An independently confirmed intervention study has not isolated them.

## Simulation and economics

Label budgets use declared workload and review assumptions. Coverage,
allocation and monitoring results concern specified simulations. No customer
review expenditure, commercial saving or production monitoring program was
measured.

Sequential behavior depends on horizon, conditional null, breach, cadence and
label supply. Historical capped-monitor results do not describe the repaired
runtime.

## Demonstration and discovery scope

A finite gate sweep and an authored injection scenario do not establish
general adversarial robustness. Exact blocked-workflow counts are not
production false-block rates.

Known grader omissions and successful repairs do not establish general
discovery yield. The later bounded discovery study found no new omission under
its frozen method.

## Released-record identifiability

The unscored-trial study does not recover original planned executions, retries,
export causes or corrected official rankings. Agreement on the strict cohort
is partly imposed by eligibility.

The stopping-feedback project did not evaluate a policy. Its feasibility STOP
is not a zero-effect estimate.

## Operational limits

Local records, hashes and replay do not establish authorship, trusted time,
deployment identity or absence of another private history. Warrant is not
an authenticated multi-tenant evidence service.

The retained core CI has a documented Linux native-policy expected failure
for an undefined entrypoint in the third-party engine (Decision 145).
An expected failure is reported explicitly and is not a passing test.

## Evidence that would close a gap

| Gap | Additional evidence |
|---|---|
| Returned-bound interpretation | Independent mathematical/source review, versioned repair and compatibility evaluation |
| Dirty historical source | Original archived patch/environment, or a separately identified clean study with disclosed differences |
| Model reconstruction | Exact weight/tokenizer revisions and extraction inputs/environment |
| Label validity | Independent blinded source adjudication under an explicit rubric |
| External validity | A preregistered evaluation on a defined independent population |
| Production readiness | Deployment study, operational controls, threat model and measured outcomes |
| Novelty | Full closest-work comparison and an evidence-backed useful distinction |

The handbook and preprint describe the present evidence. These completion
criteria are not presented as work already done.
