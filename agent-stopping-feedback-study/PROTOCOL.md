The intended intervention boundary was irreversible abandonment at one

would forfeit that run's recorded completed success; it would not prove the
intermediate patch failed.

The brief's prospective accounting, not implemented after Gate B failed, is:

```text
z_i = 1 - b_i + b_i*a_i
actual cost = sum_i[c_i + z_i*(C_i-c_i)] + monitoring overhead
retained recorded successes = sum_i z_i*Y_i
```

Here C is total execution expense and c expense already incurred. Neither is
silently replaced by text length. Both success-based denominators would be
explicitly undefined when zero. Unaudited policy results and audit-restored
successes would have to be reported separately and consistently. An evaluator
could know every saved outcome, while a simulated deployed monitor could see
labels only where z=1. None of those policy outcomes was computed here.

The brief's retention target and opportunity-cost criterion were design choices,
not industry standards or observed results. A future independent study would
need development-only choices, issue-grouped splits, duplicate/cross-corpus
checks, feasible repository holdouts, matched audit expenditure, positive
inclusion probability, fixed checkpoints and separate audit-randomization versus
future-workload uncertainty. Repeated pointwise testing would not become a
time-uniform guarantee. Gate B failed before these stages; this document does
not pretend that their requirements were satisfied.
