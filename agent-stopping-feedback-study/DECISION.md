# STOP — Gate B did not pass

The inspected releases do not support the remaining question about heterogeneous
execution expense with defensible accounting. Further investment in this study
is not justified under the current data and budget. This is a feasibility
decision, not evidence that early stopping is unprofitable.

Gate A admitted one narrow empirical question: whether feedback acquisition
after an observed executor/workload change erases the advantage of an already
useful frozen stopping policy, beyond ordinary label-sample-size effects. The
distinguishing control would homogenize remaining expense while preserving
outcomes/stop scores, and compare established uniform/stratified audits at
matched expenditure. Sampling mathematics, transfer failure and transcript-token
stopping economics already have close precedents. See [CLOSEST_WORK](CLOSEST_WORK.md).

## What actually ran

We read primary literature and public artifact inventories, pinned both requested
datasets, inspected bounded Parquet footers, and deliberately examined one
complete example from each of two source/model groups. We did not download the
corpora. Public cards had already exposed example labels; these are exploratory
convenience records, not untouched evaluation data.

Both example records contain ordered messages/tool calls and a recorded final
`resolved=0`. Neither has structured per-call usage, elapsed time or cumulative
cost. These two failures are not a success-rate estimate. The inspected typed
schemas agree about the missing accounting fields. A linked, comparably selected
two-configuration evaluation cohort remains unestablished; we stopped before
bulk cohort construction.

## Why the permitted proxies did not rescue this question

Tool counts and reconstructed transcript tokens are valid units for a narrower
descriptive replay. Missing dollar billing alone is not a failure condition.
Mixed workload shifts also need not be invalid; they must be described honestly.

Here, however, the surviving contribution concerns the joint distribution of
stopping risk and actual remaining execution expense. The representation does
not recover repeated input contexts, cache treatment, omitted reasoning,
context compaction or tool runtime. Identical recorded interaction counts can
therefore correspond to different resource costs and different cost orderings.
Tokenizing the saved text does not supply those missing observations.

A count-based audit replay would answer a different estimand. We found no
specific reason that showing audit labels consume recorded interactions would
advance beyond existing selective-label auditing and transcript-token economics.
We did not change the question after the feasibility failure to manufacture a
result. Collection filtering is an additional transport limitation, not our
sole finding or an automatic reason to reject all training corpora.

The strongest reviewer objection is that we demanded stronger accounting than
the brief required: a tool-count pilot is executable. The response is limited:
such a pilot is valid for its own unit, but it does not test the remaining
expense mechanism, and its substantive distinction was not established. This
STOP does not prove that proxy research or other public sources are inadequate.

Reconsider only with cheaply available, step-aligned usage/time or cost records
and sufficient executor/context information to define comparable expense across
eligible groups, or an independently motivated and genuinely distinct question
about interaction budgets. The latter must not be invented merely to continue
this failed study. Original continuation outcomes cannot identify restarts,
replacement agents or intermediate-patch success.
