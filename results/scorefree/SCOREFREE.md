# Certifying a detector that emits no score

Generated by `scripts/26_scorefree.py`. Every number is computed; none is typed.

> **Scope.** SIMULATED judge. Latent per-item flag probability, k independent Bernoulli draws. A real judge's runs share a prompt and a temperature and are correlated, which makes the vote fraction a worse estimate than this assumes. Every figure here is an UPPER BOUND on what k buys. It certifies no real detector.

## The finding

**A single-sample judge cannot be certified to a tight false-positive budget however much data you have.** At `k = 1` the tightest budget any useful threshold reaches is **0.105**, which is just the judge's own false-positive rate — one bit gives one usable cut point, so there is no dial to turn. The data's own floor at 20,000 negatives is 0.00015, three orders of magnitude tighter and completely unreachable.

**And the fix is cheap and bounded.** The crossover — the smallest `k` at which granularity stops binding and the benchmark's own certification capacity takes over — is `k = 3` at 200 negatives and `k = 7` at 20,000. Past it, more samples are inference spend buying nothing.

At `k = 50` the detector has 51 notches and lands on 10 distinct operating points. Buying notches does not buy operating points in proportion — which is the same finding `DECISIONS.md` 141 made about Presidio's rule-fired confidence, reached from the other direction.

## Why k belongs in the warrant key

Certify at `k = 9`: threshold 0.4444, measured false-positive rate 0.0240, certified upper bound **0.0336**.

Apply the identical threshold at `k = 1`: realised false-positive rate **0.0790** — 2.4× the certified bound. The warrant is **VOID**.

So `judge-sim@k=9` and `judge-sim@k=1` are different detectors and are keyed as such. A warrant that did not name `k` would bound a procedure nobody was running.
