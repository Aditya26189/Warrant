# Phase 37 - The incumbent check narrows the handoff hypothesis

An early 6 October primary-source check found stronger overlap with the
proposed product than the first October memo described. LangSmith documents
custom experiment-level precision and recall, issue workflows, automated
testing and shared review interfaces. Dataiku describes cross-platform
monitoring, scheduled tests and exportable risk records, while its product
page still phrases availability as future within October. None of these
sources shows a customer's configured packet or proves that Warrant's
budget-specific certificate is absent. The build decision is therefore to
keep the current local handoff and seek an actual reviewer comparison, not
to add a generic dashboard or another evaluation platform feature.

## Question and scope

The October research memo was checked on 1-3 October. The user asked for
continued work and the October plan schedules a market refresh. This phase
asked whether newer official sources or overlooked incumbent capabilities
would justify changing the next build. It used official product pages,
documentation and organizer-result searches. The source dates and the 6
October check date are kept separate. A vendor announcement is evidence of
what the vendor says it offers, not proof of field performance. Search
results are not a complete worldwide hackathon census; no new post-3
October winner was established in the targeted organizer search.

The scope is desk research and a build decision. No account was opened,
customer packet downloaded, buyer interviewed, experiment re-run or feature
implemented. The baseline comparison is `reports/AI-OPPORTUNITIES-2026-10.md`
and `reports/INCUMBENT-PACKET-COMPARISON-2026-10.md`. Both now carry dated
addenda so readers can distinguish this check from their 1-2 October findings.

## Work and findings

LangSmith's [evaluation-types guide](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation-types)
explicitly documents summary evaluators that compute aggregate precision,
recall and other custom statistics across an experiment. Its offline and
online evaluation modes and programmable evaluators are existing tools.
The [Engine v2 announcement](https://www.langchain.com/blog/langsmith-engine-v2-redteam)
dated 24 September says the service groups production-trace issues, red-teams
agents and tests fixes before human review. Some new functions are private
beta, and the stated trace and performance figures are company-reported.
The [Custom Apps announcement](https://www.langchain.com/blog/langsmith-custom-apps)
from the same date says Plus and Enterprise teams can build shared review
interfaces over LangSmith data. These official descriptions make a generic
metrics dashboard, issue queue or automated fix loop a weak new-company
claim for Warrant.

The [Dataiku Agent Management product page](https://www.dataiku.com/product/agent-management)
describes agent inventory across platforms, quality and risk monitoring,
scheduled tests, certification status and exportable records. It says the
product **will** be available in October 2026. As of the 6 October check,
neither that page nor the September announcement established the exact GA
day, a customer installation or the fields of a real exported packet. The
prior memo already warned that broad governance is incumbent territory;
this check reinforces that warning without inventing a launch milestone.

The targeted search for official winner announcements after 3 October did
not establish a later major AI competition winner to add. The existing
memo's September 30 Elastic and earlier organizer results remain the
dated competition evidence. Absence from this limited search does not mean
no newer event occurred worldwide. No hackathon prize is being used as
evidence of deployed reliability or customer willingness to pay.

The resulting narrower question is operational: for one team's existing
review trigger, does its configured incumbent packet already show a
predeclared false-positive budget, independently sourced outcome labels,
correct sampling units, selection history and enough retained data to let
another reviewer recompute the conclusion? If yes, Warrant has no shown
incremental value there. If a reviewer points to a missing field and a
recurring decision that needs it, the smallest export-specific fix may be
worth building. Product documentation alone cannot settle this comparison.

## What this does not establish

This phase does not establish that LangSmith or Dataiku either has or lacks
Warrant's exact one-sided statistical certificate in every configuration.
LangSmith supports programmable custom aggregate evaluators, so a capable
team might already implement an equivalent method. Dataiku's described
record is not a packet we have inspected. There was no matched customer
dataset, account trial, benchmark, price comparison or installation audit.
The check does not establish a new October winner, a new niche, traction,
buyer interest or a reason to fund a startup. It is a reason to tighten the
next question before writing more product code.

The synthetic clean-clone handoff and 2,215-test Phase 36 gate establish
local software behavior on their named commits. They do not turn an
unobserved customer workflow into evidence. Calling local replay a buyer
validation result would conflate implementation quality with demand.

## Open items

The October 14 gate still seeks five conversations, two permissioned usable
exports and one named recurring decision. None is recorded. A reviewer must
show the incumbent packet for the **same** threshold or review-budget
decision, including any custom evaluator rather than only the default UI.
The owner must define label provenance and the independent unit before
Warrant processes a real export. The October 8 scheduled refresh can check
for a newly dated official winner or product availability; it should not
restate this desk comparison without new evidence.

Until a concrete packet reveals a gap, the plan's freeze on speculative
adapters, dashboards, gateways and new detectors remains in force. This is
an explicit product-scope constraint, not a claim that the current CLI is
commercially proven. The next engineering change should answer a defect
observed in that reviewer trial or in production-grade validation, not an
inferred trend from a prize gallery.
