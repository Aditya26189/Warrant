**Month one costs more than steady state, and the difference is not a discount anyone can take early.** Neyman allocation needs the per-band prevalences that the first month's labels are bought to discover, so month one runs proportional at full simple-random-sampling cost. `config.yaml` carries an *expected* design effect of 0.67; applying it to a month-one plan is the silent failure `CLAUDE.md` lists as *Neyman allocation on day one*, and it under-sizes the first month by about 50% — in the one month when nobody has a prior estimate to notice it against.

## Does the interval cover what it claims?

- **96.8%** over 1,000 trials, against a nominal 95%.
- True finite-population recall 0.3333, realised prevalence 0.03000.
- Mean interval width 0.17693.

Clopper-Pearson is conservative by construction and this design adds a second conservatism by publishing an interval with no finite-population correction, so coverage is expected at or above nominal. Over-covering claims less than the evidence supports; under-covering claims more than it can.

## Allocation

- Measured design effect on the declared band structure: **0.7929** against `config.yaml`'s expected 0.67.
- Month one runs proportional, because Neyman needs the per-band prevalences the first month's labels are bought to discover.
- Proportional earns an **exact** interval; Neyman's unequal sampling fractions do not. That is what the precision costs.
